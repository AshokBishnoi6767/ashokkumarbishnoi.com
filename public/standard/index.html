The I Standard

We play by the rules.

Not because we're afraid of them.

Because we respect boundaries.

There are rules written in law.

Rules written into contracts.

Rules written into communities.

And there are rules nobody had to write down because somewhere, sometime, somebody learned what happens when they're ignored.

We respect all of them.

I don't look for loopholes.

Technically legal isn't always right.

There will always be a clever way around something.

A technicality.

A loophole.

A grey area.

A way to win while someone else pays the price.

We're not interested.

If something is technically legal but clearly deceptive, exploitative, manipulative or unfair, we step away.

Because winning through a loophole isn't much of a win.

We'd rather build trust than exploit a technicality.

Business isn't life or death.

Nature can be ruthless.

A predator eats.

A storm destroys.

A forest burns.

Nature makes difficult decisions every day.

But business?

Business isn't a matter of life and death.

So when it comes to business:

Play by the rules.

There is no mountain of revenue worth climbing by trampling everyone below it.

Ambition can be kind.

We want to grow.

We want to build something exceptional.

We want to make money.

We want to compete.

We want to win.

None of that requires cruelty.

Ambition doesn't have to be cruel.

We can negotiate hard without being dishonest.

We can compete without sabotaging.

We can demand excellence without humiliating people.

We can make difficult decisions without forgetting that people are people.

Profit matters.

This isn't a charity.

Profit keeps a business alive.

It pays people.

It funds experimentation.

It creates resilience.

It gives an organization the freedom to build something bigger.

So yes, we put profit first.

But we don't worship it.

Profit is the engine. Trust is the fuel.

Whenever we make a decision, we ask:

What's in it for the I?

Our clients.

Our people.

Our partners.

Our customers.

The business itself.

If everyone loses so that one side can win, something has gone wrong.

We don't manipulate people.

Marketing is powerful.

Technology is powerful.

Data is powerful.

Power comes with responsibility.

We don't use deception as a growth strategy.

We don't manufacture scarcity.

We don't manufacture social proof.

We don't disguise advertising as something it isn't.

We don't make leaving harder than joining.

We don't turn consent into a trap.

We don't use someone's privacy against them.

Attention should be earned.

We respect what isn't ours.

Someone else's data isn't ours.

Someone else's creative work isn't ours.

Someone else's reputation isn't ours.

Someone else's trust isn't ours.

Someone else's customer isn't ours to deceive.

Easy to access doesn't mean ours to take.

We respect intellectual property, privacy, confidential information and the work of the people and businesses around us.

We don't poke bears.

I am proactive.

But we're not aggressive for the sake of being aggressive.

We anticipate problems.

We see threats early.

We avoid unnecessary conflict.

We don't spend our energy arguing with every piece of nonsense that crosses our path.

I don't bark. I move.

When there is another path, we take it.

When there isn't—

we finish what needs finishing.

We respect what came before us.

We're not here to declare everything old useless.

Old systems exist for reasons.

Old ideas contain lessons.

Tradition can contain wisdom.

But respect doesn't mean standing still.

We take what works.

We question what doesn't.

We build what comes next.

Respect the past. Understand the present. Build the future.

We believe in every colour.

Nature isn't black and white.

Neither are people.

Neither are businesses.

There is nuance.

There are contradictions.

There are trade-offs.

Something can be beautiful and difficult.

Powerful and gentle.

Ambitious and compassionate.

I am comfortable with paradox.

We don't believe every difficult question has a simple answer.

We believe good judgment lives in the space between extremes.

We work hard.

We don't romanticize laziness.

Good intentions don't build companies.

Ideas don't execute themselves.

Eventually, someone has to do the work.

So we work.

Hard.

Intelligently.

Consistently.

And when the work is done, we go live our lives.

Work intensely. Live completely.

We pursue useful perfection.

Not the impossible kind.

The useful kind.

The kind that asks:

Can this be clearer?

Can this be better?

Can this waste less?

Can this create more value?

We don't polish things forever.

We know when to ship.

But we never stop looking for the next improvement.

Better is a direction, not a destination.

We own our mistakes.

Something went wrong?

Own it.

Something could have been better?

Improve it.

Don't know the answer?

Say so.

Made the wrong call?

Learn from it.

Responsibility isn't punishment.

It's ownership.

We don't expect people to be perfect.

We expect them to take responsibility for what happens next.

We don't waste things.

Money.

Time.

Talent.

Attention.

Energy.

Data.

Opportunities.

Resources.

Nature has an incredible instinct for efficiency.

So do we.

Make the most of what you have.

Waste isn't just expensive.

It's unnecessary.

Above everything...

We want to build a business we'd be proud to belong to.

Profitable.

Ambitious.

Demanding.

Kind.

Funny.

Focused.

Responsible.

Unapologetically driven.

But never needlessly cruel.

I am walking green flags.

We don't claim to be perfect.

We claim to keep improving.

We don't claim to know everything.

We claim to keep learning.

We don't claim every decision will be easy.

We claim to make the hard ones with our principles intact.

The I Rule

When faced with a choice, ask:

Can we do it?

Then:

Should we do it?

Then:

Would we be comfortable explaining it publicly?

Then:

Would we still do it if there were no loophole?

If the answer isn't right—

Don't do it.

Simple.

This is the standard.

Not the standard required to avoid trouble.

Not the standard required to look good.

Not the standard required to put a nice sentence on a website.

The standard we want to hold ourselves to when nobody is watching.

Because that's when standards actually matter.

We make brands fly.

Not by stepping on people.

Not by bending every rule.

Not by playing dirty.

We fly by building something worth flying for.
