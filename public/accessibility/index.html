Accessibility Statement

Everyone should be able to use the internet.

The internet should work for people—not make them work around it.

Ashok Kumar Bishnoi is committed to making ashokkumarbishnoi.com as accessible, usable and understandable as reasonably possible for people with disabilities.

We design and improve our digital experiences with accessibility in mind and work to meet the accessibility requirements that apply to our business.

Accessibility isn't an afterthought.

It's part of building things properly.

Our approach

We consider accessibility across the way people perceive, navigate, understand and interact with our digital experiences.

Our approach considers four fundamental principles:

Perceivable

Information should be available in ways people can perceive.

Operable

People should be able to navigate and interact with the experience.

Understandable

Content, interfaces and interactions should be clear and predictable.

Compatible

Digital experiences should work with commonly used assistive technologies and user agents where reasonably possible.

Where applicable to I, we aim to align our public website with WCAG 2.0 Level AA requirements and applicable Canadian accessibility legislation.

We may also consider newer accessibility standards and recognized best practices as they become relevant to our website and operations.

What we're working to provide

We aim to make our website easier to use by providing:

meaningful alternative text for relevant images;

sufficient colour contrast;

logical headings and page structure;

keyboard-accessible navigation;

accessible forms, links and controls;

captions or alternatives for relevant multimedia;

clear and readable content;

visible focus indicators;

compatibility with commonly used assistive technologies; and

accessible alternatives when a particular format cannot reasonably be made accessible.

The goal is simple.

Make the experience work for more people.

Accessibility is ongoing.

A website doesn't become accessible once and stay that way.

New pages are published.

Content changes.

Technologies change.

Campaigns launch.

Third-party tools are added.

Any of these can introduce new barriers.

That's why we treat accessibility as an ongoing process of finding, fixing and improving.

Build it. Test it. Improve it.

Third-party content

Some content, functionality or services available through our website may be provided by third parties.

We don't control how third-party systems are designed or maintained.

Where third-party content or functionality is within our influence, we will take reasonable steps to identify accessibility barriers and seek appropriate alternatives where reasonably possible.

Accessibility requirements vary.

Accessibility requirements depend on factors such as:

jurisdiction;

organization size;

industry or sector;

the nature of the service; and

applicable legislation.

I will comply with accessibility requirements that apply to its operations.

For example, Ontario's Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) establishes accessibility requirements for certain organizations, including specific website accessibility requirements for qualifying organizations with 50 or more employees.

We do not claim that a particular statutory requirement applies to I unless the applicable legal criteria are satisfied.

We follow the rules that apply to us—and keep improving beyond them where we can.

We're not going to pretend we're perfect.

Accessibility is a continuous effort.

We may not catch every barrier immediately. When we discover one, our aim is to understand it, address it and improve the experience.

Because accessibility isn't about claiming perfection.

It's about taking responsibility for the experience we create.
