The problem

A visitor lands on your homepage. They have somewhere between eight and twenty seconds of patience, they are probably comparing you to two other companies in adjacent tabs, and they are looking for one thing: is this for me?

Most B2B technology websites answer a different question. They answer what are we? — in the vocabulary the company uses internally, arranged in the order the product team built things, with the most impressive capability given the most space regardless of whether anyone buys because of it.

The result is a page that is factually correct and commercially useless. And because the site is technically working — traffic arrives, pages load, forms exist — the diagnosis usually lands somewhere else. More traffic. More ads. More content. A new SDR.

Meanwhile the actual cost compounds quietly. Every paid click lands on a page that under-explains. Every SDR spends the first six minutes of a call doing what the website should have done. Every demo starts a step behind. And a percentage of your best-fit buyers — the technical evaluators, the ones who read carefully — leave with a slightly wrong idea of what you sell and never come back to correct it.

Rewriting the site doesn't fix a bad product or a bad market. It fixes the gap between what your product is worth and what a stranger can tell in twenty seconds.

Who this is for

  • SaaS startups whose product has outgrown the copy written when the company was three people and one idea.
  • Technology SMEs selling something genuinely complicated to a mix of technical and commercial buyers who need different things from the same page.
  • Companies launching a product or entering a new category, where nobody is searching for what you do yet because it doesn't have a name.
  • Companies whose website is accurate and confusing — the most common and least diagnosed version of this problem.

It is a poor fit if you are hoping a copywriter will substitute for a positioning decision the leadership team has not made. I can facilitate that decision. I cannot make it for you.

What I do

I start where most copy projects don't: with the product itself. Documentation, demo, pricing page, sales deck, the objections your team hears most, and wherever possible a conversation with someone who bought.

Then I work out the three or four things your buyer must understand, in order, before anything else you want to say has value. That order is the real deliverable. The sentences are downstream of it.

Positioning around clarity and buyer understanding, not word count. A shorter page that lands beats a longer page that covers everything.

Deliverables

  • Homepage
  • Product pages

  • Messaging framework — the shared story the whole company can use

Delivered as structured copy documents with section headings, hierarchy and notes for design and development, so nothing gets lost between the document and the build.

Process

  1. Immersion

    Product walkthrough, documentation, competitor pages, existing analytics if you have them, and the objections sales hears most often. Usually a week.

  2. Message before layout

    What the company claims, for whom, against what alternative, backed by what proof. Agreed in writing before a single page is drafted — this is where disagreements are cheap to resolve.

  3. Drafting

    Page by page, in priority order, so you can see the approach on the highest-stakes page before the rest is written.

  4. Revision

    Two rounds included. Consolidated feedback works far better than serial comments from five stakeholders, and I'll ask you to organise it that way.

  5. Handover

    Final copy with structure notes, alternative headline options where a choice is genuinely close, and a short rationale for the decisions that will get questioned later.

Proof

Questions

Do you write the whole website or individual pages?

Both. Some engagements are a single high-value page such as a homepage or a new product page. Others are a full rewrite across ten to twenty pages with a messaging framework underneath them so the story stays consistent.

Do you work from an existing messaging framework?

If you have one, yes. If you don't, building a working one is usually part of the engagement, because page copy written without an agreed message just moves the disagreement onto the page.

How technical can you go?

Comfortable with API documentation, architecture diagrams, security models and product demos as source material. A computer science background plus seven years writing across SaaS, AI, data, cybersecurity and IT.

Will you promise a conversion-rate increase?

No. Anyone quoting you a specific conversion lift before seeing your traffic, your funnel and your sales process is guessing. Clarity is the deliverable; you and I both watch what it does.

Send me the page you're least happy with

Include the product, the buyer, and what you wish the page did that it currently doesn't. I'll tell you whether this is a copy problem or something further upstream.
