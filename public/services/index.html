Content services for B2B technology companies

Five services. One underlying job: make a complicated product easy to understand and hard to ignore.

Most of my clients are technology companies between roughly ten and two hundred people. They have a real product, a small marketing team, and something genuinely difficult to explain. That combination is where generalist content fails most expensively — not because the writing is bad, but because the writer never understood the product well enough to know what mattered.

You don't need all five of these. You probably need one, done properly, before the others are worth discussing.

Not sure which one?

  • Website & Product Copy

    • Homepages
    • Product pages
    • Solution pages
    • Messaging

How engagements usually run

Nothing here requires a twelve-month commitment to find out whether it works.

  1. Diagnosis

    A content audit, a website review, or a strategy session. Short, paid, and useful on its own — you end up with a document you could hand to a different writer entirely.

  2. A first project

    Website and product messaging, a technical content sprint, a case study, a whitepaper, or a thought-leadership package. One defined deliverable with a defined job.

  3. A partnership

    Monthly technical content, a thought-leadership retainer, or strategy plus execution. Most of my longer relationships started as a single project that worked.

  4. Fractional content partner

    For small technology teams that need someone owning content decisions rather than filling orders. Strategy, execution and editorial judgement, without hiring a full-time content lead.

Pricing depends on scope and is discussed directly rather than published as a package price, because the same deliverable can be three days of work or three weeks depending on how much source material exists.

Start with the problem, not the deliverable

The most useful first message isn't "we need six blog posts a month." It's what you're trying to change, what you've already tried, and what happened.

I'll tell you which of these five services fits — or that none of them do yet.
