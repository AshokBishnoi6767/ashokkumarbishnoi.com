Human + Automation

A more precise way to think about what automation should target — not roles, but the specific low-judgment tasks quietly consuming skilled people's time.

Ashok Kumar Bishnoi·September 11, 2026·5 min read
Executive summary

The most defensible case for automation was never "replace the person." It was "stop wasting a skilled person's time on the parts of their job that never needed a person at all." A more precise way to think about what automation should target — not roles, but the specific low-judgment tasks quietly consuming skilled people's time.

01 — The pattern

Automation Shouldn't Replace People. It Should Replace Bad Use of People.

The public conversation about automation tends to frame it as a question about people: will this role be automated away? That framing, while understandable, obscures a more precise and more useful way to think about the same question — automation shouldn't be evaluated role by role. It should be evaluated task by task, and the honest target was never "replace this person." It was "stop asking this person to spend their time on work that never actually needed their judgment."

Input

List the tasks inside a role,…

List the tasks inside a role, not just the role itself. Break a job into its actual components and ask, task by task, which ones require the specific judgment this person was hired for.

02 — The framework

What actually closes it

a connected network of the real sequence — list the actual tasks, automate the low-judgment ones, redirect the recovered time.

Output

Automate the low-judgment components explicitly

Automate the low-judgment components explicitly, and be transparent about why — not as a step toward eliminating the role, but as a step toward using the person's actual skill more fully.

Output

Redirect the recovered time deliberately

Redirect the recovered time deliberately, toward the higher-judgment work that was always the real reason the role existed, rather than letting the recovered time simply evaporate into more low-judgment work of a different kind.

03 — Diagnostic

Where to look first

  1. Think of your most skilled team member. What's the task in their role that most clearly doesn't need their specific skill — and what would change if it were automated tomorrow?
