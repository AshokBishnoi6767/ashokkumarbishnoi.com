Overview

A smaller ecosystem, a specific reputation

Waterloo, Kitchener and Cambridge — usually referred to together as Waterloo Region, or "Tri-Cities" — sit about 100km west of Toronto. The region is small compared to Toronto's technology sector, but it has a specific, long-standing reputation in Canadian tech: a disproportionate number of engineers, founders and B2B software companies for its size.

That reputation is grounded in two real things, not marketing: the University of Waterloo's co-operative education engineering and computer science programs, and the legacy of BlackBerry (Research In Motion), which was founded and headquartered in Waterloo and built a generation of local engineering talent before its decline as a consumer smartphone company.

The ecosystem

What actually drives it

Three things show up consistently when you look at why companies form and stay here:

  • Talent pipeline. The University of Waterloo is widely regarded as one of Canada's strongest engineering and computer science schools, with mandatory co-op placements that put students inside real companies before they graduate.
  • Founder legacy. Engineers who came up through BlackBerry/RIM went on to start or join a wave of smaller companies across the region, a pattern common in tech hubs that formed around one large employer.
  • Proximity to Toronto. Waterloo Region is close enough to Toronto (roughly a 90-minute drive) to draw on its capital and customer base, while operating with lower costs than the city itself.

Waterloo Region is sometimes described in Canadian tech media as "Canada's Silicon Valley" — a nickname, not a ranking. It reflects a reputation for producing technical talent relative to its population, not a claim that it rivals Toronto, Vancouver, or Silicon Valley in company count or capital raised.

Notable companies

A few real examples — not a ranked list

This is a small, illustrative set of B2B SaaS companies with real roots in the region, spanning different categories. It is not exhaustive, not ranked, and not a claim about which is "best" — see the sourcing note at the bottom of this page.

ED-TECH

ApplyBoard

Founded in Waterloo in 2015, ApplyBoard operates a platform connecting international students, recruitment partners and educational institutions — a B2B marketplace model serving the education sector.

SALES & MARKETING

Vidyard

Founded in Kitchener in 2010, Vidyard provides video hosting, management and analytics tools built for B2B sales and marketing teams.

CONSTRUCTION TECH

Bridgit

A Waterloo Region-based company building workforce planning and resource management software for the construction industry — a clear example of vertical, industry-specific B2B SaaS.

Why this region matters

Not the biggest. Specific for a reason.

Waterloo Region won't out-raise or out-hire Toronto, and it isn't trying to. What it consistently offers is a tighter loop between a strong engineering school, a base of experienced technical founders, and lower operating costs than a major city core — while staying close enough to Toronto's capital and enterprise customers to matter commercially.

For B2B SaaS specifically, that combination tends to favor vertical, problem-specific products over broad horizontal platforms — which lines up with the examples above: ed-tech, sales enablement, and construction workforce management, not general-purpose enterprise suites.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers

Is Waterloo Region actually a real B2B SaaS hub, or just a marketing phrase?

It's real, if smaller than Toronto. Waterloo Region's tech identity was built on the University of Waterloo's co-op engineering and computer science programs and the legacy of BlackBerry (Research In Motion), which was founded and headquartered in Waterloo. That combination has continued to produce technology founders and engineers, including B2B SaaS companies such as ApplyBoard and Vidyard.

What kinds of B2B SaaS companies come out of Waterloo, Kitchener and Cambridge?

The region doesn't have one dominant category. Notable companies span ed-tech platforms (ApplyBoard), B2B video and sales-enablement tools (Vidyard), and construction workforce-planning software (Bridgit) — reflecting a broader pattern of vertical, problem-specific SaaS rather than one industry cluster.

How does Waterloo Region compare to Toronto for B2B SaaS?

They're complementary, not competing. Toronto has far more capital, larger enterprise customers, and a bigger overall company count. Waterloo Region has a tighter talent pipeline relative to its size, lower operating costs, and close proximity to Toronto (about a 90-minute drive), which is why many companies end up with a presence in both.
